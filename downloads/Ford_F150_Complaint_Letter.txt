Re: Formal Complaint – 2025 Ford F-150 XLT 4x4
    Purchased December 24, 2025


To Whom It May Concern,

The purpose of this letter is to formally document the issues we have experienced with our 2025 Ford F-150 XLT 4x4, leased new on December 24, 2025.

We leased this vehicle to replace our 2021 F-150, whose warranty was expiring and which had experienced repeated air-conditioning issues. We also had an upcoming road trip to Alabama during the Christmas holidays and wanted to ensure we were traveling in a dependable, trouble-free vehicle.

We took delivery on December 24 and departed Fort Lauderdale for Alabama on December 26. Approximately two hours into our drive, while traveling at highway speed, the vehicle began to shudder violently. The episode lasted approximately 40–45 seconds. During this event, the accelerator did not respond properly; depressing the pedal caused the shuddering to intensify while the speed decreased.

We immediately contacted our sales representative and were told the issue could be a flat spot in a tire that would correct itself. Based on my prior mechanical experience, I knew the symptoms were not consistent with a tire flat spot. Shortly afterward, the issue occurred again, and we captured video of the event and forwarded it to the dealership. We also received vehicle alerts indicating a misfire and advising that service was required.

The dealership reviewed the video and advised us to bring the vehicle to a Ford service department for diagnostic testing. Due to the holiday period, no dealerships in Georgia were able to schedule us before January 4. We therefore continued to Alabama.

While in Alabama, additional issues occurred, including:

    •  Multiple misfire events
    •  An instance where the electronic brake system displayed an error
       and would not release
    •  A complete freeze and malfunction of the Apple CarPlay system

As no dealership in Alabama could accept the vehicle immediately, we made the decision to drive home at reduced speeds, avoiding highways. Despite these precautions, we experienced approximately 30 misfire events during the return trip.

Upon returning to Fort Lauderdale, we brought the vehicle to the dealership on January 2. After a two-day inspection, we were informed that the misfire could not be duplicated. No corrective action was taken regarding the reported tire vibration above 70–85 mph, and we were again told the flat spot would resolve itself. By that point, the vehicle had approximately 1,800 miles.

On the drive home from that service visit, the vehicle misfired on the highway again. We immediately contacted the dealership and returned the vehicle on January 4. It is currently in service.

We have been informed that the dealership is attempting to diagnose the issue and that replacement parts may not arrive until late March, with an estimated completion in April.

Waiting approximately four months for resolution on a brand-new vehicle purchased December 24 is unacceptable. Additionally, my wife is currently 28 weeks pregnant. Given the repeated misfire events, braking malfunction, and drivability concerns, we no longer feel safe operating this vehicle.

While we remain loyal Ford customers and supporters of the brand, we are not comfortable continuing with this particular vehicle. We are requesting a prompt and appropriate resolution, whether that be vehicle replacement or another suitable remedy under applicable consumer protection laws.

Please contact us at your earliest convenience to discuss next steps.
