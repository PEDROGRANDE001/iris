# Outdoor & Performance Fabrics for Hospitality FF&E

*Last updated: 2026-03-29*

## Fiber Types — The Big Three

### 1. Solution-Dyed Acrylic (SDA)
**The gold standard for outdoor hospitality.**

- **How it works:** Color is mixed into liquid acrylic *before* the fiber is extruded. Color goes all the way through — like a carrot vs. a radish.
- **UV resistance:** 2,000+ light hours. Colorfastness rating of 8/8.
- **Fade warranty:** Typically 5–10 years
- **Lifespan:** 8–10 years with proper care
- **Key properties:** Soft hand feel, breathable, water-resistant (not waterproof), mildew-resistant, bleach-cleanable
- **Chlorine/salt resistance:** Excellent — critical for beach clubs and pool decks
- **Drawbacks:** Higher cost ($25–$80/yd depending on brand), limited pattern range compared to printed polyester
- **Best for:** Cushions, umbrellas, daybed fabrics, anything in direct sun with pool/ocean exposure

### 2. Solution-Dyed Polyester (SDP)
**The mid-range option.**

- **UV resistance:** Moderate — typically 1,000–1,500 light hours
- **Fade warranty:** 1–3 years
- **Key properties:** More affordable, good water resistance (especially when coated), decent color retention
- **Drawbacks:** Fades faster than acrylic in direct sun, less breathable, can become brittle over time
- **Best for:** Covered/shaded areas, throw pillows, items that rotate seasonally

### 3. Olefin (Polypropylene)
**Budget-friendly performer.**

- **UV resistance:** Good but below SDA
- **Fade warranty:** 2–5 years
- **Key properties:** Quick-drying, naturally stain-resistant, excellent moisture/mildew resistance, lightweight
- **Drawbacks:** Rougher texture, limited color range, **not fire-resistant** (breaks down under high heat — important for hospitality fire code compliance)
- **Best for:** Covered patios, poolside sling chairs, budget-conscious projects
- **⚠️ Fire code concern:** Olefin's low heat resistance can be a deal-breaker for hotels/restaurants that must meet strict fire codes.

### Other Options
- **PVC-Coated Polyester (Vinyl):** 100% waterproof, commercial-grade durability, heavy, less breathable. Good for covers, sling chairs, heavy-use commercial seating. Brands: Phifertex, Twitchell, Herculite.
- **Printed Polyester:** Wide pattern options but only 500–1,000 light hours UV resistance. Not recommended for permanent outdoor hospitality installations.

## Technical Specifications — What Matters for Contract/Hospitality

### Abrasion Resistance (Wyzenbeek Double Rubs)
Per ACT (Association for Contract Textiles) guidelines:

| Application | Minimum Wyzenbeek | Minimum Martindale |
|------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Low traffic (hotel rooms, suites) | 15,000 double rubs | 20,000 cycles |
| High traffic (lobbies, restaurants, pools) | 30,000 double rubs | 40,000 cycles |
| 24-hour use (casinos, ER, terminals) | >30,000 (often 50,000+) | >40,000 |

**Note:** Wyzenbeek and Martindale are NOT correlated — you can't convert between them. Results above 100,000 double rubs don't reliably predict longer life.

**Sunbrella typical performance:** ~40,000 double rubs (awning/marine grade)

### Colorfastness to Light
Per ACT standards:
- **Upholstery (indoor/outdoor):** AATCC 16 Opt. 3 — Grade 4 minimum at 40 hours
- **Drapery:** AATCC 16 Opt. 3 — Grade 4 minimum at 60 hours
- **Coated upholstery:** Grade 4 minimum at 200 hours, or ASTM D4329 no change at 150 hours
- **Outdoor upholstery:** ASTM G154 (xenon arc weathering)

### Flammability
Critical for hospitality — check local codes, but common requirements:
- **CA TB 117-2013** (California Technical Bulletin) — standard for upholstered furniture
- **NFPA 260** — cigarette ignition resistance for upholstery
- **NFPA 701** — flame propagation for drapery
- **ASTM E84** — surface burning characteristics for wall coverings

### Other Key Tests for Outdoor Hospitality
| Test | Standard | What It Measures |
|------|----------|-----------------|
| Water repellency | AATCC 22 (spray) | Water beading on surface |
| Hydrostatic resistance | AATCC 127 | Pressure needed to push water through |
| Fungal/mildew resistance | ASTM G21 | Growth of fungi on fabric |
| Crocking (color transfer) | AATCC 8 | Wet/dry rubbing color transfer |
| Tear strength | ASTM D2261 | Resistance to tearing |
| Breaking strength | ASTM D5034 | Tensile strength before failure |
| Pilling resistance | ASTM D3512 | Surface pilling under abrasion |

## Fabric Selection Guide for Beach Club / Pool Environments

### Full Sun + Pool/Ocean Exposure (Daybeds, Loungers, Cabana Cushions)
**→ Solution-Dyed Acrylic (Sunbrella, Outdura, or Sattler)**
- Must handle chlorine splash, salt spray, sunscreen, body oils
- Bleach-cleanable is essential for hospitality housekeeping
- 5+ year fade warranty minimum
- Budget: $30–$65/yd for fabric alone

### Covered Areas (Bar Seating, Covered Dining)
**→ SDA preferred, SDP acceptable**
- Reduced UV exposure means SDP can perform adequately
- More pattern/design flexibility with SDP
- Consider FibreGuard for high-traffic dining (stain-free tech)
- Budget: $20–$50/yd

### Sling Furniture (Pool Deck Loungers, Dining Chairs)
**→ PVC-coated polyester mesh (Phifertex, Twitchell)**
- Quick-dry, breathable, no cushion needed
- Antimicrobial treatment important for pool areas
- Extremely durable under heavy commercial use
- Budget: $15–$40/yd

### Umbrellas & Shade Canopies
**→ Marine-grade SDA**
- Heavier weight (9+ oz/sq yd) for structural integrity
- Tighter weave for water resistance
- Must handle wind loads
- Budget: $25–$50/yd

## COM/COL Considerations for Hotel Brand Standards

When specifying COM (Customer's Own Material) or COL (Customer's Own Leather) for major hotel brands:

- **Ritz-Carlton / Marriott Luxury:** Typically require ACT-certified fabrics. Minimum 30,000 double rubs for public areas. Fire code compliance mandatory. COM must ship to manufacturer with proper yardage + overage (usually 10–15%).
- **Four Seasons:** Known for strict durability standards. Expect requests for extended abrasion testing. Prefer established brands (Sunbrella, Perennials).
- All luxury brands require **full test reports** with COM submissions — not just spec sheets.

**Hidden costs to factor:**
- Cutting waste: 10–20% overage depending on pattern repeat
- Welting/piping fabric: Often different grade/color
- Freight from fabric supplier to upholstery manufacturer
- Fire retardant treatment (if fabric isn't inherently FR)
- Testing fees: $500–$2,000+ for full ACT panel

## Key Takeaways for Higney International

1. **Default to Sunbrella SDA** for any full-sun beach club/pool application. It's the safe spec that every manufacturer knows and every hotel brand accepts.
2. **Consider Sattler** as a cost-saving alternative — essentially identical performance at 15–20% less. Good for value-engineering conversations.
3. **Outdura** is another solid Sunbrella alternative with strong sustainability story.
4. **Always check fire codes** for the specific jurisdiction — Miami-Dade has its own requirements beyond state code.
5. **For Ritz-Carlton Bal Harbour:** Expect to provide full ACT test reports. Minimum 30,000 double rubs. All outdoor fabrics should be bleach-cleanable.
6. **Sling furniture for pool decks** — Phifertex or Twitchell mesh is more practical than cushioned furniture for high-turnover areas.
7. **Budget 10–15% fabric overage** on all COM orders for cutting waste and damage replacement.
