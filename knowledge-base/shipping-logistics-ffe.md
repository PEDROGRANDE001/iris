# Shipping & Logistics for FF&E Furniture

*Last updated: April 7, 2026*

## Container Types & Dimensions

### Standard Containers

| Container | Interior Dimensions | Cubic Capacity | Payload | Pallets |
|-----------|-------------------|----------------|---------|---------|
| **20' (TEU)** | 19.4' × 7.7' × 7.9' | 1,172 cu ft (33.2 m³) | 55,127 lbs (25,000 kg) | 9–10 standard |
| **40' (FEU)** | 39.5' × 7.7' × 7.9' | 2,389 cu ft (67.7 m³) | 61,200 lbs (27,600 kg) | 20–21 standard |
| **40' High Cube (HC)** | 39.5' × 7.7' × 8.9' | ~2,694 cu ft (76.3 m³) | ~58,600 lbs | 20–21 standard |

**Key insight:** A 40' container has double the space of a 20' but typically costs only **20–25% more**. Almost always the better value if you can fill it.

### Specialty Containers (relevant to FF&E)

- **Open-Top:** For oversized pieces that can't fit through standard doors (e.g., large cabanas, oversize lounge structures). Loaded by crane from above.
- **Flat Rack:** For extra-wide or irregularly shaped items (e.g., large custom installations, pergola frames). Collapsible sides.
- **High Cube (HC):** Extra foot of height (9'6" exterior). Essential for tall items like bar backs, large parasols, or stacked lounge furniture.

## FCL vs. LCL: Decision Framework

### Full Container Load (FCL)
- **Pricing:** Flat rate per container
- **Current rates (early 2026):**
  - Asia → US West Coast: ~$2,000–2,500/FEU
  - Asia → US East Coast: ~$3,000–3,500/FEU
  - Europe → US East Coast: ~$1,500–3,200/FEU
- **Best for:** Orders filling >50% of a container; high-value/fragile items; tight deadlines
- **Advantages:** Fewer handling touchpoints, lower damage risk, faster transit, no consolidation delays

### Less Than Container Load (LCL)
- **Pricing:** Per cubic meter (CBM)
- **Current rates (early 2026):** ~$100–150/CBM from Asia
- **Best for:** Shipments under 12–15 CBM; sample orders; fill-in items
- **Disadvantages:** More handling = more damage risk; consolidation adds 5–10 days; higher per-unit cost above 15 CBM

### Breakeven Rule of Thumb
- **Under 10 CBM:** LCL is 40–60% cheaper
- **10–15 CBM:** Compare closely — LCL may still win
- **15+ CBM:** FCL almost always wins on cost-per-unit
- **25–30 CBM:** FCL is definitively cheaper

**For Higney International:** Most project orders (Ritz-Carlton, Four Seasons scale) will fill containers. Use LCL for samples, urgent fill-ins, or small accessory orders.

## Incoterms 2020: What Matters for FF&E

The 11 Incoterms define who pays for what and when risk transfers. The most relevant for furniture procurement:

### Most Common for FF&E Imports

| Term | Who Arranges Shipping | Risk Transfers | Best For |
|------|----------------------|----------------|----------|
| **EXW (Ex Works)** | Buyer handles everything from factory door | At factory | When you have a strong freight forwarder and want full control |
| **FOB (Free On Board)** | Seller delivers to vessel; buyer handles ocean freight | When goods are on board | Most common for sea freight — good balance of control and simplicity |
| **CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight)** | Seller pays freight + minimum insurance to destination port | When goods are on board at origin | When supplier has better freight rates; buyer still handles import customs |
| **DDP (Delivered Duty Paid)** | Seller handles everything including import duties | At buyer's door | Turnkey — but you lose control and visibility; seller bakes costs into price |

### Recommendations by Scenario

- **Established manufacturers (Vietnam, Indonesia, China):** Use **FOB** — gives you control over shipping costs and forwarder selection
- **European suppliers (Turkey, Italy, Spain):** **CIF** or **DAP** can work well — shorter routes, suppliers often have competitive freight rates
- **First-time suppliers or small orders:** Consider **DDP** to reduce risk until relationship is established
- **Letter of Credit transactions:** Use **CIF**, **CIP**, or **FCA** (with onboard B/L notation per 2020 update)

### Critical Details
- Always specify the **exact named place** (e.g., "FOB Ho Chi Minh City Cat Lai Terminal" not just "FOB Vietnam")
- Only CIF and CIP require seller-provided insurance. For all others, **arrange your own cargo insurance**
- Insurance typically runs **0.3–0.5% of cargo value** for all-risk coverage

## US Customs & Duties for Furniture (Current as of Q1 2026)

### Standard MFN (Most Favored Nation) Duty Rates

| HTS Code | Product | Base MFN Rate |
|-----------|---------|---------------|
| **9401.61** | Upholstered seats with wooden frames | Free |
| **9401.71/79** | Metal-frame seating | Free |
| **9401.80** | Other seats | Free |
| **9403.20** | Other metal furniture | Free |
| **9403.40** | Wooden furniture for kitchens | Free |
| **9403.60** | Other wooden furniture | Free |
| **9403.70** | Plastic furniture | Free |
| **9403.89** | Furniture of other materials (rattan, bamboo) | Free |
| **9405** | Lighting fixtures | Free–3.9% |
| **6306.12** | Synthetic fiber tarpaulins/awnings | Free–4.7% |

**Note:** Most furniture enters the US duty-free under MFN rates. The cost impact comes from **additional tariffs** layered on top.

### Section 232 Tariffs on Wood Products (Effective Oct 14, 2025)

| Product | Rate (2026) | Scheduled Increase (Jan 1, 2027) |
|---------|-------------|----------------------------------|
| Upholstered wooden furniture | **25%** | 30% |
| Kitchen cabinets & vanities | **25%** | 50% |
| Softwood timber & lumber | **10%** | — |

**Country exemptions:**
- **UK:** Capped at 10%
- **EU & Japan:** Capped at 15%
- Products from these countries are significantly cheaper to import

### Section 301 Tariffs (China-specific)

| Product Category | Additional Duty on Chinese Goods |
|-----------------|----------------------------------|
| Metal furniture (HTS 9403.20) | **35% total** (Free MFN + 10% + 25% Section 301) |
| Most other furniture from China | **25%** Section 301 on top of MFN |

**Strategic implication:** This is why Vietnam, Indonesia, and Turkey have become dominant sourcing alternatives. Chinese metal furniture at 35% duty vs. free from Vietnam is a massive cost differential.

### Reciprocal Tariffs (IEEPA, as of 2025–2026)
- Additional tariffs vary by country under various Executive Orders
- Products already under Section 232 wood tariffs are **exempt** from overlapping IEEPA/reciprocal tariffs
- Check current rates — this landscape changes frequently

### Customs Bond Requirement
- **Continuous bond:** Required for any importer bringing in >$2,500/year in goods
- **Cost:** Typically $500–1,000/year for bonds up to $50,000
- **Single-entry bond:** ~$50–100 per shipment (for occasional imports)
- Higney International should maintain a **continuous customs bond**

| Origin → Destination | Sea Freight | Air Freight |
|---------------------|-------------|-------------|

| Vietnam → US West Coast | 18–25 days | 4–6 days |
| Indonesia → US East Coast | 30–40 days | 5–8 days |
| China → US East Coast | 25–35 days | 3–5 days |
| China → US West Coast | 14–20 days | 2–4 days |
| Turkey → US East Coast | 20–30 days | 4–6 days |
| Italy → US East Coast | 18–25 days | 3–5 days |

**Add to sea freight times:**
- Customs clearance: 2–5 business days (standard); longer if inspected
- Drayage to warehouse: 1–3 days
- Deconsolidation & inspection: 1–2 days
- **Total door-to-door: Sea + 5–10 days for last mile**

## Packaging & Crating Best Practices for FF&E

### General Rules
1. **Custom crating** for anything over $500 value — generic cardboard is inadequate for ocean freight
2. **ISPM 15 compliance** mandatory for all wood packaging (heat-treated, stamped) — non-compliant wood gets your container held at port
3. **Desiccant packets** inside containers to prevent moisture/mold during 3–5 week ocean transit
4. **Photo documentation** before packaging for insurance claims

### By Product Type

| Item | Packaging Method |
|------|-----------------|
| **Outdoor lounge furniture (metal/aluminum)** | Stretch wrap + foam corners + cardboard sleeves. Stack with cardboard dividers. Cushions vacuum-packed separately. |
| **Upholstered seating** | Plastic stretch wrap (moisture barrier) → foam padding on arms/legs → moving blankets → secure with straps. Do NOT stack heavy items on top. |
| **Glass/marble tabletops** | Foam sheets between layers, custom A-frame crate for vertical shipping. "FRAGILE" + "THIS SIDE UP" labeling. |
| **Parasols/umbrellas** | Individual cardboard tubes or crates. Fabric wrapped separately from poles. |
| **Lighting fixtures** | Individual foam-lined boxes. Double-wall corrugated minimum. Extra foam at attachment points. |
| **Daybeds/cabanas** | Disassemble. Wrap frames in foam + moving blankets. Fabric/curtains shipped in sealed bags with desiccant. |

### Container Loading Tips
- Heavy items on bottom, lighter on top
- Fill ALL voids with dunnage (lumber, airbags, foam) — cargo shifts at sea destroy unbraced loads
- Cargo must withstand **lateral pressure up to 70% of vertical stacking weight** (containers tilt up to 45° in rough seas)
- Use plywood or lumber between tiers
- Moisture protection: plastic shrouds over top and sides of stacked cargo

## Total Landed Cost Calculation

When quoting a project, calculate the **true landed cost** per item:

```
Landed Cost = Product FOB Price
            + Ocean Freight (per unit allocation)
            + Marine Insurance (~0.3–0.5% of value)
            + Customs Duties (MFN + any Section 301/232)
            + Customs Broker Fee ($150–250 per entry)
            + Terminal Handling Charges (THC) — $200–400/container
            + Drayage (port to warehouse) — $300–800 depending on distance
            + Deconsolidation/warehousing
            + Last-mile delivery to job site
```

**Rule of thumb for budgeting:** Add **15–25%** to FOB price for freight, duties, and handling on standard routes from Asia. For items subject to Section 232/301 tariffs, add **40–60%**.

## Demurrage & Detention: The Hidden Budget Killers

- **Demurrage:** Fees for leaving your container at the port terminal past free time (typically 3–5 free days)
  - Rates: $75–250/day, escalating
- **Detention:** Fees for holding the container past allowed time after pickup
  - Rates: $75–200/day
- **Per diem/chassis:** If using a chassis, additional daily rental

**Prevention:**
- Have warehouse space ready BEFORE container arrives
- Clear customs paperwork in advance (ISF filing 24+ hours before vessel loading)
- Coordinate drayage pickup for day 1–2 of free time
- Budget $500–1,000 contingency per container for delays

## Insurance

### Marine Cargo Insurance
- **Institute Cargo Clause A (All Risk):** Covers almost everything except war, strikes, inherent vice. **Use this for FF&E.**
- **Institute Cargo Clause C (Basic):** Named perils only — too restrictive for furniture
- **Cost:** 0.3–0.5% of declared cargo value (negotiable with volume)
- **Always insure at CIF value + 10%** (standard practice to cover lost profit)

### When to File Claims
- Document damage at delivery with timestamped photos
- Note damage on Bill of Lading / delivery receipt BEFORE signing
- File within carrier's claim window (typically 9 months for ocean, shorter for domestic)
- Keep all packaging materials until claim is resolved
